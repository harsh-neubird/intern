## DNA structure
DNA is a double helix: two strands wound around each other. Each strand is a chain of nucleotides with four bases: adenine (A), thymine (T), guanine (G) and cytosine (C). A pairs with T, and G pairs with C. The double helix structure was described by Watson and Crick in 1953, building on X-ray images by Rosalind Franklin.

## Chromosomes
Human body cells have 46 chromosomes, arranged in 23 pairs. One set of 23 comes from each parent. Sperm and egg cells have 23 chromosomes. The 23rd pair are the sex chromosomes: XX or XY.

## Genes and the genome
A gene is a stretch of DNA that codes for a protein or a functional RNA. The human genome has about 3 billion base pairs and roughly 20,000 protein-coding genes. The Human Genome Project finished its first complete draft in 2003.

## DNA replication
Before a cell divides, it copies its DNA. Replication is semi-conservative: each new DNA molecule keeps one original strand and gains one new strand. The enzyme DNA polymerase builds the new strand.

## RNA
RNA is usually single-stranded and uses uracil (U) instead of thymine. Messenger RNA carries the gene's instructions from the nucleus to the ribosomes.
