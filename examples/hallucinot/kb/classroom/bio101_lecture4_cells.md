## Cell theory
All living things are made of one or more cells. The cell is the basic unit of life. All cells come from pre-existing cells. Cell theory was developed in the 1830s and 1850s by Schleiden, Schwann and Virchow.

## Prokaryotes vs eukaryotes
Prokaryotic cells (bacteria and archaea) have no nucleus; their DNA sits in a region called the nucleoid. Eukaryotic cells (animals, plants, fungi, protists) have a membrane-bound nucleus and membrane-bound organelles. Prokaryotic cells are usually much smaller, about 1 to 10 micrometers across.

## Mitochondria
Mitochondria produce most of the cell's ATP through cellular respiration. They are found in nearly all eukaryotic cells, including both animal and plant cells. Mitochondria have their own small circular DNA, which supports the endosymbiotic theory that they descended from free-living bacteria.

## Chloroplasts
Chloroplasts carry out photosynthesis, turning light energy, water and carbon dioxide into sugar and oxygen. They are found in plant cells and algae, not in animal cells. Like mitochondria, chloroplasts have their own DNA.

## Cell membrane and cell wall
Every cell has a cell membrane made of a phospholipid bilayer that controls what enters and leaves. Plant cells also have a rigid cell wall made of cellulose outside the membrane. Animal cells do not have a cell wall.

## Ribosomes
Ribosomes build proteins by reading messenger RNA. They are found in all cells, both prokaryotic and eukaryotic, either free in the cytoplasm or attached to the rough endoplasmic reticulum.
